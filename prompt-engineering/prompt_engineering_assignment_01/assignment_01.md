## Analysis

**Which techniques you used, and where in your prompts you used them**

- Role - I started the prompt with the role description, so that it would have enough weight on the prompt overall.
- Context - I added "relevant information" as bullet points directly after describing the task itself.
- Constraints - I defined a "Rules" section for all constraints, after giving context for the task.
- Few-shot - I wrote examples of outputs directly after the context and constraints, so that the model had considered that information before responding.
- Format - Putting format last allowed the model to square away everything else under the specified structure.

**How each technique affected the output. Did it improve, stay the same, or get worse? Point to specific differences between versions**

- Role - Gives a more curated, focused response, which improved the output. The difference I noticed for the water bottle was the two sections which briefly highlighted the "work" of the "teams" behind the bottle. This made the pitch feel more grounded and reliable.
- Context - Virtually removed the model's ability to creatively invent what it would talk about in relation to the product's features. Honing down the list kept the pitch focused and improved overall.
- Constraints - Giving a word count constraint had a dramatic impact on output size, regardless of whether it actually matched the desired word count or not. Some of the earlier larger outputs because very minimal and short. I was able to greatly impact the tone of the water bottle pitch here as well, when I asked it to use light, easy-going language.
- Few-shot - Giving an example of the tone I was shooting for gave the model something to run with, and it improved the reading towards what I imagined.
- Format - This was the highest-impact technique, because adjusting the layout/structure changed the overall flow of the pitch. With the format no longer undefined, every technique could be better represented.

**Any times the model ignored part of your prompt or made up details you didn't provide, and what you changed in response**

- For the standing desk, the model used language stronger than I anticipated, so I asked it to change its tone to be lighter.
- Other than that, no model made up details that weren't included in the prompt, other than the initial prompt outputs.

**Which technique made the biggest difference for you, and why you think that was**

- Format was the gamechanger. Being able to define the structure and layout of the output allowed me to control and be intentional about all the other prompt-engineering decisions I'd already made. I could span out the features, highlighting some, or squash the necessary down into a brief paragraph. The ideal output for any input could be made the best or the worst depending on the format.
